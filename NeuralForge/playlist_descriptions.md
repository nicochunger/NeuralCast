# NeuralForge Playlist Definitions

This is the editorial reference for song selection and playlist placement. Each playlist heading exactly matches its CSV filename under `playlists/`. Read the target definition and its neighboring playlists before proposing additions, moves, or repetition cleanup.

Existing rows provide examples, but may contain historical placement mistakes. Described periods are centers of gravity rather than automatic cutoffs. Judge the particular recording and its listening role, not just the artist, nationality, release year, or appearance in a film.

Personal enjoyment and each playlist's listening character guide selection; representative genre coverage supports that purpose rather than requiring encyclopedic completeness. Melody, atmosphere, and engaging songwriting are recurring priorities, but do not imply a blanket preference for soft music or clean vocals. Automatic new-release additions and unusually high artist counts are not, by themselves, evidence of personal favorites.

## Placement and overlap

- For additions, search the station catalog first, including alternate artist credits, punctuation, and version labels. Prefer one strongest playlist home; repeat only recordings fundamental to both concepts, subject to the explicit Aspen Vibes and Rock Nacional exceptions below.
- NeuralCast's Aspen Vibes intentionally overlaps other genres and periods for well-known soft-ish listening. Rock Nacional intentionally preserves representative Argentine scene coverage, including reggae crossovers. These are editorial exceptions, not mistakes to remove automatically.
- NeuralForge's New Releases may temporarily overlap permanent genre playlists while releases remain in the active window. Cross-station overlap is evaluated independently because the stations serve different audiences.
- Distinct covers, live performances, mixes, and language versions are separate recordings; verify the media before treating name variants as duplicates.
- Definitions guide proposed edits; they do not authorize unrelated cleanup. Follow `AGENTS.md` for CSV validation and coordinated playlist-local CSV/MP3 changes.

## Playlist catalog

### Celtic Metal

Metal centered on Celtic folk character, traditional melodies, pipes, regional folk expression, and mythic atmosphere, with Skiltron and Triddana illustrating its melodic side and Primordial and Saor its darker, atmospheric range. The existing selection reaches into neighboring regional folk traditions, so Celtic identity is the center rather than a nationality test; an artist's imagery alone is insufficient. Use Folk Metal for broader festive or pagan selections, and assess uncertain boundary cases individually rather than treating every existing row as a precedent.

### Classic Metal

Traditional heavy metal, speed metal, and thrash with an Iron Maiden, Megadeth, and Metallica backbone, supported by essential songs from Sabbath, Priest, Dio, Motörhead, Accept, and their peers. Deep cuts, long compositions, and later recordings by favorite bands belong alongside familiar classics; this is substantial exploration of those bands rather than evenly weighted historical coverage. Keep rock-led songs in Hard Rock and use NWOBHM when discovery of the specific scene is the stronger selection reason.

### Epic Cinematic Orchestral

Melodic, emotionally engaging instrumental music for fantasy, adventure, and cinematic listening, with Antti Martikainen, Two Steps From Hell, and Thomas Bergersen as reference points. Heroic peaks, wonder, folk-colored fantasy, and quieter atmospheric passages all belong; dramatic musical arcs matter more than constant volume or trailer-style impact. This orchestral listening lane complements the station's fantasy/metal atmosphere and remains distinct from band-led Symphonic Metal and NeuralCast's broad classical canon.

### Fantasy Metal

Escapist metal built around quests, mythical worlds, theatrical adventure, and exuberant fantasy, with Twilight Force, Evertale, Power Paladin, and Eons Enthroned as reference points. Epic power and symphonic arrangements are frequent foundations, and playful excess or deliberate silliness, as in ANGUS McSIX, can be part of the appeal. A convincing fantasy atmosphere matters alongside literal lyrical themes; use Power Metal or Symphonic Metal for ordinary genre staples without that identity, and retain overlap only where both concepts are fundamental.

### Folk Metal

Metal with recognizable folk melodies, traditional instruments, and regional song forms, balancing communal drinking, dancing, and adventure songs with darker atmospheric pagan epics. Korpiklaani, Alestorm, Turisas, and Equilibrium establish the festive, adventurous center; Moonsorrow, Månegarm, and Arkona provide a more expansive, darker counterweight, with both harsh and clean vocals welcome. Prefer this home over Melodic Death Metal when folk character dominates, and use Celtic Metal when Celtic melodies, pipes, or related traditional atmosphere are the stronger selection reason.

### Folk Rock

An energetic, communal mix of pub singalongs, Celtic punk, shanties, and medieval festival music, with Dropkick Murphys, Flogging Molly, the Pogues, and the Real McKenzies alongside Santiano, dArtagnan, and Versengold. Traditional singalong roots, pirate songs, humor, and reflective folk songs belong when they serve this listening character; this is not a general singer-songwriter folk-rock collection. Folk melodies and the communal or festival role guide selection, while recordings whose main appeal is their metal weight fit Folk Metal more strongly.

### Hard Rock

Familiar, melodic guitar rock spanning hard rock, arena rock, and selected softer classics, with strong hooks and memorable songwriting as the connecting thread. AC/DC, Thin Lizzy, Scorpions, and Ghost share space with Boston, Journey, Foreigner, ballads, and selected alternative-rock songs; forceful riffs are one part of the range rather than a requirement for every recording. Prefer Classic Metal for traditional heavy metal and thrash, and do not broaden this definition to justify isolated extreme-metal placements.

### Melodic Death Metal

Melodic death metal favoring epic melodies, atmosphere, emotional weight, and expansive arrangements, with Aether Realm, Brymir, and Wintersun alongside Insomnium, Omnium Gatherum, and Amorphis as reference points. Harsh vocals and death-metal weight are welcome, but aggression alone is not the attraction; memorable riffs, harmonized guitars, and engaging musical development should carry the selection, including foundational Gothenburg material. Folk, symphonic, and progressive elements belong when the melodic-death foundation remains strong; use their dedicated playlists when those elements define the particular song more strongly.

### NWOBHM

Discovery of the New Wave of British Heavy Metal scene beyond its biggest surviving bands, with a broad selection of lesser-known acts and their distinctive songs. The late 1970s/1980s breakthrough is the historical center, but later recordings by scene bands can continue their representation rather than being excluded by date. Preserve the scene focus when evaluating uncertain entries: British nationality, an old-fashioned sound, or later influence alone is insufficient, and the broader Classic Metal playlist remains the home for general heavy-metal coverage.

### Neo Classical Metal

Classically influenced metal and melodic virtuosity, with Malmsteen, Jason Becker, Cacophony, and Vitalij Kuprij anchoring the classical-composition and adaptation strand. A secondary guitar-showcase strand includes selected work by Paul Gilbert, Chris Poland, and Shawn Lane, but technical accomplishment alone does not make every recording neoclassical; vocal songs should have a clear musical reason to belong. Distinguish this focus from Prog Metal's developed songwriting and Symphonic Metal's orchestral drama, and do not treat a large number of automatically added tracks by one artist as the playlist's taste reference.

### New Releases

A rotating discovery playlist for recent releases relevant to NeuralForge's metal, hard-rock, folk, and epic musical scope. Selection and aging are governed by the new-release pipeline, including companion metadata, rather than a permanent historical definition. Temporary overlap with a core genre playlist is intentional during the active release window; preserve that behavior when reviewing repetitions.

### Power Metal

Melodic power metal centered on expressive singing, memorable choruses, and dramatic storytelling, with Helloween, Sonata Arctica, and Stratovarius as reference points alongside more martial and theatrical anthems. Emotional ballads and melodic mid-tempo songs belong alongside fast, uplifting material: speed and constant triumphalism are not requirements. Symphonic and progressive touches fit when the song retains a strong power-metal center; use Fantasy Metal when escapist world-building is the stronger identity, and do not repeat a song in Prog Metal solely because its band sometimes uses complex arrangements.

### Prog Metal

Progressive metal centered on memorable melodies, expressive vocals, atmosphere, and developed songwriting, with Dream Theater, Seventh Wonder, and Circus Maximus as the main reference points. Strong vocal hooks and dramatic arrangements should carry the playlist; complexity and instrumental skill fit when they serve an engaging song, and melodic instrumentals can also belong. Keep a smaller representative selection of heavier and technical approaches, but do not expand Tool, Opeth, or Haken merely for completeness: the owner's enjoyment takes precedence over encyclopedic genre coverage. Prefer Power Metal for straightforward melodic anthems and Neo Classical Metal for virtuosity-led songs when progressive composition is secondary; do not repeat songs across these playlists solely because a band spans genres.

### Symphonic Metal

Metal combining expressive singing, memorable melodies, gothic atmosphere, and orchestral drama, with Nightwish, Within Temptation, Tarja, Epica, Xandria, and Blackbriar as reference points. Intimate, emotional songs belong alongside large epics, and vocals can range from operatic to clean or harsh; orchestration should support an engaging song rather than qualify a recording by itself. Keep the band and symphonic elements central, and repeat a song in Power Metal only when it is fundamental to both listening concepts.

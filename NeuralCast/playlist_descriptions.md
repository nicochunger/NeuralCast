# NeuralCast Playlist Definitions

This is the editorial reference for song selection and playlist placement. Each playlist heading exactly matches its CSV filename under `playlists/`. Read the target definition and its neighboring playlists before proposing additions, moves, or repetition cleanup.

Existing rows provide examples, but may contain historical placement mistakes. Described periods are centers of gravity rather than automatic cutoffs. Judge the particular recording and its listening role, not just the artist, nationality, release year, or appearance in a film.

## Placement and overlap

- For additions, search the station catalog first, including alternate artist credits, punctuation, and version labels. Prefer one strongest playlist home; repeat only recordings fundamental to both concepts, subject to the explicit Aspen Vibes and Rock Nacional exceptions below.
- NeuralCast's Aspen Vibes intentionally overlaps other genres and periods for well-known soft-ish listening. Rock Nacional intentionally preserves representative Argentine scene coverage, including reggae crossovers. These are editorial exceptions, not mistakes to remove automatically.
- NeuralForge's New Releases may temporarily overlap permanent genre playlists while releases remain in the active window. Cross-station overlap is evaluated independently because the stations serve different audiences.
- Distinct covers, live performances, mixes, and language versions are separate recordings; verify the media before treating name variants as duplicates.
- Definitions guide proposed edits; they do not authorize unrelated cleanup. Follow `AGENTS.md` for CSV validation and coordinated playlist-local CSV/MP3 changes.

## Playlist catalog

### Acoustic Singer-Songwriter

Intimate, lyric-led songs built around acoustic instruments, expressive vocals, and spare arrangements, from folk foundations to contemporary singer-songwriters. Prefer stripped-back storytelling over glossy radio production; a song belongs here because of its performance and arrangement, rather than simply being a ballad. Aspen Vibes accommodates the more polished, familiar soft-radio counterpart.

### Aspen Vibes

Soft rock is the core, with a broader selection of well-known songs that feel soft-ish, melodic, warm, or comfortable to listen to across genres and periods. Adult contemporary, familiar pop ballads, gentle indie, and melodic rock all belong; the former 1980–2000 range is not a cutoff. Overlap with other playlists is intentional here: retain familiar soft-ish songs even when another playlist is their primary genre or era home, and consider removal only for clear energy or mood outliers such as driving party music or urgent, abrasive rock.

### Bossa Nova

Brazilian bossa nova and its jazz crossover: understated samba rhythms, gentle guitar, relaxed vocals, and lyrical instrumental performances associated with Jobim, João Gilberto, and their collaborators. Keep its Brazilian rhythmic identity distinct from Evening Jazz, which is organized around a late-night jazz mood. Different landmark interpretations of a standard can be worthwhile when the performances are distinct.

### Christmas Jingles

Recognizable Christmas songs, festive standards, and seasonal favorites, from crooners and early rock and roll through soul and contemporary holiday recordings. Despite the name, this is a song playlist rather than a collection of station jingles. Holiday relevance can justify overlap with another playlist when the recording is fundamental to both seasonal and musical traditions.

### Classic Pop

Enduring mainstream pop with immediately recognizable hooks, centered on the 1970s–1990s and extending to established crossover classics around the turn of the millennium. Dance-pop, synth-pop, vocal pop, and signature radio ballads fit; select songs for lasting pop identity rather than routing every famous rock song here. Millennial Nostalgia is the stronger home for songs primarily remembered as a late-1990s/2000s cultural moment.

### Classic Reggae

Foundational reggae, roots, rocksteady, and lovers rock, together with established melodic reggae crossover hits. Bob Marley and the Jamaican pioneers anchor the playlist, while UB40, Maxi Priest, Jimmy Cliff, and similar accessible singers broaden it beyond a strict roots-era cutoff. Favor tuneful vocals, memorable melodies, and widely known recordings when filling gaps; later hits may belong here if their musical identity is classic reggae rather than contemporary reggae production.

### Classic Rock

Foundational guitar-led rock, centered on the 1960s–1980s with defining 1990s and selected turn-of-the-millennium classics. Blues rock, hard rock, arena rock, melodic rock, and landmark grunge fit; a few foundational metal crossovers belong alongside Zeppelin, Queen, the Stones, AC/DC, and their peers. Prefer Classic Pop for synth-pop and glossy pop ballads, Millennial Nostalgia for era-led pop-rock, and the dedicated prog playlists for borderline progressive tracks; repeat only truly central rock/prog or rock/metal landmarks.

### Contemporary Pop

Widely recognized mainstream pop from roughly 2010 onward, including radio ballads, dance-pop, and major contemporary crossover singles. Recognition and a clear pop-song identity matter more than a particular production style. Use Latin Pop for primarily Latin repertoire and Indie Vibes for songs whose independent/alternative identity is stronger than their chart-pop placement.

### Cuarteto

Córdoba's cuarteto tradition and its popular Argentine party repertoire, from La Mona Jiménez and Rodrigo to contemporary groups. Dance rhythm, regional identity, and collective singalong appeal define the selection. Include current songs that carry that tradition, rather than using a fixed historical cutoff or treating all Argentine dance music as interchangeable.

### Cumbia Villera

Argentine cumbia villera and closely connected bailanta staples, with direct vocals, prominent keyboards, dance rhythms, and neighborhood storytelling. Damas Gratis, Yerba Brava, and related popular acts provide the center, with recognizable adjacent cumbia tracks where they fit the same listening context. Preserve this local scene identity rather than widening it into a general Latin dance playlist.

### Deep House

House music grounded in a steady groove, warm bass, soulful or jazz-influenced textures, and club-oriented repetition, from foundational Chicago recordings to later deep-house productions. Prefer rhythmic depth and a sustained house feel over large trance builds or pop chorus drops. Melodic EDM covers the more sweeping emotional journey, while Tropical House covers lighter summer-pop production.

### Eclectic Discovery

Exploratory songs combining funk, soul, hip-hop, jazz, electronic, and alternative influences, with an emphasis on distinctive grooves and unexpected combinations. Anderson .Paak, Kaytranada, Flying Lotus, and similar boundary-crossing artists illustrate the direction. This is a discovery lane, not a catch-all for unrelated viral tracks or a second home for every Indie Vibes song.

### Evening Jazz

Relaxed, expressive jazz suited to late-night listening: melodic small-group performances, restrained instrumental standards, and selected vocal jazz. Favor atmosphere, phrasing, and musical warmth over maximal intensity, while retaining essential performances by artists such as Miles Davis and Bill Evans. Use Bossa Nova when the Brazilian style itself is the defining feature.

### Folklore Argentino and Chamame

Representative Argentine folk traditions, including song-centered folklore and the regional dance and instrumental language of chamamé. Mercedes Sosa, traditional singers, and chamamé ensembles share a home through roots, place, and cultural recognition. Select repertoire for its folk tradition and performance, keeping tango, romantic pop, and rock in their dedicated homes.

### Funk & Soul

Groove-centered funk, soul, and closely related classic R&B, anchored in the 1960s–1980s and open to later recordings that strongly carry the tradition. Expressive singing, bass and drum interplay, brass, and danceable rhythmic character guide placement. A famous soul or funk recording should normally stay here rather than being repeated in Classic Pop solely because it was a hit.

### Global Classics

Songs with lasting international recognition that crossed language and cultural boundaries, spanning European standards, African and Middle Eastern crossovers, Asian landmarks, and Latin American and Caribbean classics. Geographic breadth and cultural familiarity define the selection, including signature international dance and novelty hits. Avoid turning it into a second general English-language pop catalog; a song needs a clear global or cross-cultural reason to belong.

### Golden HipHop

Recognizable hip-hop classics centered on the late 1980s–2000s, including boom-bap, West Coast rap, Southern breakthroughs, and defining crossover singles. MC performance, storytelling, production, and influence on hip-hop are the core criteria. Prefer this genre home over Millennial Nostalgia for ordinary era overlap, reserving repetition for recordings central to both concepts.

### Heavy Metal Anthems

A compact selection of metal's most recognizable riffs, choruses, and cultural landmarks, spanning foundational heavy metal through landmark thrash, alternative, and nu-metal. This is NeuralCast's accessible metal entry point rather than an exhaustive subgenre collection; NeuralForge carries the deeper metal repertoire. Classic Rock overlap is appropriate for a few foundational recordings that define both rock-radio and metal traditions.

### Indie Vibes

Melodic independent and alternative pop/rock, predominantly from the 2000s onward, balancing memorable songs with distinctive textures and modest experimentation. Arctic Monkeys, Tame Impala, Fleet Foxes, and contemporary indie songwriters illustrate its scope. Familiar gentle tracks can also fit Aspen Vibes, while genre-bending groove experiments belong more naturally in Eclectic Discovery.

### Irish Folk

Irish traditional music, folk songs, acoustic storytelling, and modern performances that clearly carry Irish folk instrumentation or phrasing. Traditional ensembles, ballad singers, and folk-informed crossover groups fit; being Irish is not sufficient on its own. Pop, soul, ambient, or rock songs by Irish artists belong in their musical or mood home unless the particular recording has a strong folk identity.

### Latin Pop

Widely known Spanish-language and Latin crossover songs, from romantic ballads and melodic pop-rock to dance hits, bachata-pop, reggaeton, and contemporary urban pop. The 1990s/2000s are a major foundation, but older essentials and later major hits also belong. Prefer Rock Nacional for songs primarily representing Argentine rock, Reggae Argentino for Argentine reggae, and Cuarteto or Cumbia Villera for those local styles; retain only fundamental crossovers in both.

### Melodic EDM

Electronic music led by melody, atmosphere, and emotional progression, including melodic/progressive house, trance, and related sweeping instrumental or vocal productions. Above & Beyond, Ben Böhmer, Chicane, and similar artists anchor the direction; popularity alone does not make every dance or novelty hit a fit. Prefer Deep House for groove-led house and Synthwave for recordings whose retro-synth identity is stronger.

### Mid-Century Popular Foundations

Foundational popular music from the swing and crooner eras through rhythm and blues, early rock and roll, and enduring early-pop standards. The center is the 1930s–early 1960s, with nearby landmark recordings included when they carry that historical tradition. Choose actual performances and their cultural role, keeping different covers distinct and placing later pop nostalgia primarily in Classic Pop.

### Millennial Nostalgia

Widely recognized songs that defined growing up in the 1990s and 2000s, spanning pop, pop-punk, alternative rock, hip-hop, dance, and crossover hits. Playful novelty songs, Eurodance earworms, and big singalong party tracks such as Crazy Frog, Vengaboys, and Scatman are an intentional part of the identity. The former Y2K label and 1998–2003 range were too narrow; prefer this home for era-led hits, while repeating genre staples only when they are truly fundamental to both playlists.

### Modern Reggae

Contemporary reggae and melodic reggae fusion, including roots revival, lovers-style singing, accessible reggae-pop, and prominent international or Latin American reggae artists. Chronixx, Tarrus Riley, Koffee, Cultura Profética, and Gondwana illustrate the range beyond Jamaica alone. Prioritize melodic artists and songs; use Reggae Argentino for that national scene, Classic Reggae for established classic-style repertoire, and Reggae Rock for rock-led/coastal-band arrangements.

### Movie and TV Soundtracks

Recognizable film and television themes, score cues, and songs strongly identified with an iconic screen moment or soundtrack. A signature theme, an original film song, or a defining soundtrack recording is a stronger fit than a familiar radio song merely licensed for a scene. Retain overlap with another playlist only when the recording is fundamental to both musical and screen identities.

### Neo-Prog

Melodic progressive rock rooted in the 1980s neo-progressive revival, with expressive vocals, rich keyboards, narrative songwriting, and extended arrangements. Marillion, IQ, Pendragon, Arena, and related later torchbearers define the lineage. This is a style rather than a date label: newer progressive artists belong in Progressive Rock unless their sound clearly follows the neo-prog tradition.

### Progressive Rock

Melodic and atmospheric progressive rock built around developed arrangements, instrumental interplay, and longer musical arcs, centered on the classic era and open to appropriate modern successors. Favor the approachable, reflective side of Pink Floyd, Genesis, Yes, Camel, and related artists while allowing essential energetic landmarks. Use Neo-Prog for that specific revival tradition, and avoid repeating borderline prog tracks in Classic Rock merely because they are well known.

### Punk Rock

Punk, pop-punk, and ska-punk spanning first-wave pioneers through later melodic and regional scenes. Direct songwriting, rhythmic urgency, rebellious character, and recognizable scene staples guide selection, including Ramones, The Clash, Green Day, NOFX, and Ska-P. Millennial Nostalgia overlaps should be limited to a few defining generational anthems rather than copying whole runs of pop-punk hits.

### Reggae Argentino

Representative Argentine reggae, with particular emphasis on melodic singing, romantic hooks, and widely recognized songs from Los Cafres, Los Pericos, Dread Mar I, Nonpalidece, and their peers. Roots and scene-adjacent tracks fit when they clearly carry the local reggae sound. Overlap with Rock Nacional is intentional when a recording is important to its representative Argentine catalog; foreign Spanish-language reggae belongs more naturally in Modern Reggae.

### Reggae Rock

Reggae blended with rock, ska, alternative, or coastal-band songwriting, from Sublime and 311 to Rebelution, Iration, Dirty Heads, SOJA, and Stick Figure. Warm grooves, tuneful hooks, and an accessible melodic feel are central, with upbeat rock energy where it serves the song. Choose by the recording's arrangement rather than putting all reggae-pop here; classic reggae and roots-led contemporary singers have their own homes.

### Rock Nacional

A representative Argentine rock and alternative catalog, centered on the major bands and songs of roughly the 1980s–2010s with relevant foundations and successors. Its national-scene breadth intentionally includes reggae, ska, pop-rock, and other crossover bands that are important to Argentine rock culture. Preserve that representation even when songs also fit Reggae Argentino or another genre playlist; do not remove entries merely to minimize cross-playlist repetitions.

### Romanticismo Argentino

Argentina's classic romantic idols and melodic popular songs, centered on the 1960s/1970s tradition of Sandro, Leonardo Favio, Palito Ortega, and their peers. Theatrical balladry, youthful longing, and familiar local melodies matter more than a strict genre boundary. Keep its historical Argentine romantic identity distinct from modern Latin Pop, tango, and the broader soft-radio selection in Aspen Vibes.

### Soft Metal

The reflective and melodic side of metal and closely related hard rock: power ballads, acoustic passages, restrained performances, and emotional slow-building songs. Choose the specific recording's softer arrangement, rather than treating every track by a metal artist as eligible. A few landmark power ballads can be core to both this playlist and Classic Rock or Aspen Vibes.

### Symphonic Classics

Recognizable orchestral and classical repertoire, from canonical composers to modern orchestral recordings and selected classical crossover performances. Focus on melodic familiarity and the orchestral or classical listening experience rather than screen association alone. Movie and TV Soundtracks is the stronger home for score cues whose primary identity is a particular film; crossovers belong here when their classical/orchestral character is central.

### Synthwave

Retro-futurist electronic music shaped by 1980s synth timbres, cinematic atmosphere, neon nostalgia, and modern production. Com Truise, The Midnight, FM-84, and related instrumental or vocal acts illustrate the direction. This is a contemporary retro style rather than a container for all original 1980s synth-pop, which generally belongs in Classic Pop.

### Tango

Representative Río de la Plata tango songs and instrumental performances, from foundational standards and dance orchestras to important later developments. Buenos Aires identity, phrasing, bandoneón, and the tradition's dramatic storytelling guide selection. Distinct landmark interpretations may coexist; Argentine nationality or romantic lyrics alone do not make a pop or folk song tango.

### Tropical House

Relaxed, accessible house and dance-pop with a sunny feel, light percussion, melodic vocal hooks, and tropical-style instrumentation. Kygo, Bakermat, Felix Jaehn, and suitable adjacent radio-house recordings set the listening direction. Keep it lighter and more song-oriented than the sweeping builds of Melodic EDM or the sustained club grooves of Deep House.
